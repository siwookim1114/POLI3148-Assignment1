In Mali, if post-coup violence were jihadist resurgence, non-state killings would be rising. Instead, they are holding flat. Stanton's argument fits this asymmetry: actors with command structures escalate, while the groups the regime claims to fight do not. Her further distinction between selective violence (regimes with control) and indiscriminate violence (regimes losing it) places Mali on the latter side [Stanton 2016]. The pattern matches Hultman's strategic-targeting logic [Hultman 2007] and the Wagner-enabled escalation Marten and McCullough & Sandor describe [Marten 2023; McCullough & Sandor 2023].
